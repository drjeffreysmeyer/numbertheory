Proposition 5.3.4. Eisenstein’s Lemma.

If \(p\) is an odd prime and \(a\) is an odd integer relatively prime to \(p\text{,}\) then \(\gamma(a,p) \equiv \beta(a,p) \pmod{2}\text{.}\)

🔗

Hint.

Try a direct proof. Rewrite and compare two sums: \(\sum_{r=1}^{(p-1)/2} r\) and \(\sum_{r=1}^{(p-1)/2} ar\text{.}\) First, carefully show that
\begin{equation*} \sum_{r=1}^{(p-1)/2} r = \sum_{r=1}^{(p-1)/2} C_a(r) = p \beta(a,p) + \sum_{r_a < \frac{p}{2}}r_a - \sum_{r_a > \frac{p}{2}}r_a. \end{equation*}
Recall that, for each \(r\) with \(1 \leq r \leq (p-1)/2\text{,}\) \(ar = p \left\lfloor \frac{ar}{p} \right\rfloor + r_a\) (why???). With this in mind, show that
\begin{equation*} \sum_{r=1}^{(p-1)/2} ar = \sum_{r=1}^{(p-1)/2} \left(p \left\lfloor \frac{ar}{p} \right\rfloor + r_a\right) = p \gamma(a,p) + \sum_{r=1}^{(p-1)/2} r_a. \end{equation*}
Look at their difference to deduce that
\begin{equation*} (a-1)\sum_{r=1}^{(p-1)/2} r = p (\gamma(a,p)-\beta(a,p)) + 2\sum_{r_a > \frac{p}{2}}r_a. \end{equation*}
Deduce the result. You may find Definition 5.1.4, Proposition 5.1.5, Definition 5.1.6, Definition 1.1.1, Proposition 2.1.6, and Definition 2.4.1, useful.
